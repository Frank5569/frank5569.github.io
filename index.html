The memory tier between HBM and SSD, explained from device physics to market impact

High Bandwidth Flash stacks 3D NAND dies on a logic base die and places them beside an AI accelerator, promising HBM-class read bandwidth with roughly ten times the capacity. Sandisk and SK hynix published the first open HBF specification through the Open Compute Project in 2026, and the first devices are expected around 2027. This book is a quantitative, independent assessment of what HBF can and cannot do, and what it means for AI inference and the memory industry.

  • How HBF reaches terabytes per second through plane-level parallelism, and what limits it
  • Read disturb, retention, endurance, ECC and reliability compared with conventional 3D NAND
  • Why large-model decode inference fits HBF while training does not, and which parts of the KV cache can move to flash
  • Stack fabrication, TSVs, wafer bonding and yield; the OCP HBF specification and four ways to attach HBF to an accelerator
  • A disaggregated inference cost model: sweep time, accelerator count and cost per token
  • Market impact, adoption and case studies of Sandisk, SK hynix, Samsung, Micron, Kioxia, YMTC, Huawei and CXMT
  • A timeline, a risk register and an outlook for AI memory and inference once HBF ships in volume

Extensive derivations and analytical calculations, with references to published papers and patents. Written for memory and storage engineers, system and accelerator architects, researchers, and technology and market analysts.

Random forests, gradient boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost and stacking are the workhorses of machine learning on industrial tabular data. This book explains how they work, and how to use them where the data are hardest: semiconductor fabs and the storage fleets of AI data centers.

  1. Part IThe essentials. Why averaging and boosting reduce error, how to combine models, and how to validate, calibrate and monitor them the way they will actually be deployed.
  2. Part IIFab data. Hierarchy, drift, few labels and rare fails, and wafer-map defect-pattern recognition.
  3. Part IIIStorage fleets. A paper-by-paper review of the published field studies of HDD and SSD failure prediction, asking which results would survive deployment.

Every chapter ends with a summary, questions for practitioners and annotated references. Written for engineers and data scientists who need models they can trust, explain and maintain.
